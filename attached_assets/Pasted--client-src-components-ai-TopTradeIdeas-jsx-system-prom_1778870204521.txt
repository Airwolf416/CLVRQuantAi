# `client/src/components/ai/TopTradeIdeas.jsx` — system prompt addendum

The hardener cleans up the back end. But it can only catch and mutate; it can’t make the LLM smarter on the first pass. The cleanest gains come from tightening the system prompt that gets built at lines 156–180.

## Add these rules to the existing system prompt string

Inject this block into the prompt builder, ideally near the top of the “rules” section so the LLM sees it before reasoning:

```text
═══ ABSOLUTE RULES — violations cause card rejection ═══

NO SUPERLATIVES without ranked-comparison data. The Market Snapshot does NOT
contain cross-asset OI/volume rankings. Therefore the following are banned:
"largest", "biggest", "highest", "most", "standout", "exceptional",
"unprecedented", "leading". Use neutral language: "elevated", "notable",
"positive", "significant relative to its own history".

OI SCOPE. Any OI value you cite is for the SUBJECT symbol only. Do not
compare it to other assets. ($1.02B is not "the largest in crypto" — BTC
perp OI is ~$30-60B.)

REGIME CONSISTENCY. The Market Snapshot contains the macro regime from the
classifier. Your thesis MUST use that exact regime. Do not say "RISK-OFF"
when the snapshot shows "MACRO_CLEAR". The user sees the regime banner.

SAMPLE-SIZE HONESTY. If statisticalBrain n < 30, you MUST write the literal
phrase "small sample (n=X)" in the thesis. Never call low-n results
"statistically significant" or describe them as "a real edge".

FUNDING CALIBRATION. Funding rates between -0.01% and +0.01% per 8h are
NEAR-FLAT. Do not call this "trending", "momentum", "consistent with
directional flow", or "institutional positioning". It means little.

CHASE DISCLOSURE. If you are proposing LONG after a +4% or larger 24h move,
or SHORT after a -4% or larger 24h move, you MUST write the phrase
"this is a late entry chasing" in the thesis and downgrade conviction
accordingly.

NUMERIC CONSISTENCY.
- Stop distance from entry MUST be at least 1.5x ATR(1h). The ATR is in
  the Market Snapshot. If your proposed stop is tighter, widen it.
- RR to TP1 MUST be at least 1.8:1. If you can't get that with a valid
  stop, do not generate the card.
- TP prices MUST be on the correct side of entry for the direction.
```

## Why this matters even with the hardener

The hardener is the safety net, not the strategy. If the LLM’s first-pass output is closer to correct, three things improve:

1. Fewer veto-drops → more cards survive to the UI
1. Fewer material mutations → fewer prose-regen calls → lower latency + cost
1. The displayed thesis matches the displayed numbers more often, even when the hardener didn’t trigger regen

## What this does NOT fix

The LLM still invents the numbers from scratch. A prompt cannot guarantee the LLM correctly computes 1.5x ATR or correctly checks RR — it’ll usually get close but sometimes drift. That’s why the hardener exists. But raising the floor is cheap.

## Optional: also pass the hardener thresholds explicitly

Consider injecting the actual numeric thresholds the hardener will apply:

```text
The post-generation hardener will REJECT cards meeting any of:
- ATR-multiple stop < 1.8x (stop too tight)
- RR to TP1 < 1.8:1
- Post-haircut conviction < 50%
- Chase + crowded simultaneously

Cards that meet these floors but are mutated by the hardener (stop widened,
conviction haircut) will have their thesis regenerated, costing latency.
Generate cards that pass the hardener cleanly.
```

This turns the hardener into a known constraint surface the LLM optimizes against, rather than a black box that randomly nukes its work.
